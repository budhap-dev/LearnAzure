## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] Two revenues, one board meeting
> The data is flowing ([lesson 10.3](lesson:10.3)): orders are mirrored, stock arrives hourly, and analysts have started building reports. At the September board meeting the finance director quotes revenue of £1.42m. Marketing's deck, on the next slide, says £1.51m.
>
> Both are "right". Finance's report excludes VAT and cancelled orders. Marketing's adds up the order totals in the mirrored tables, cancellations and all. Each wrote its own SQL against raw data, and nobody knew the other had.
>
> Priya asks Tom for one definition of revenue, built once, that every report uses. Tom thinks in T-SQL. Kofi, the data engineer who joined last month, thinks in Python and Spark. They need to agree where the model lives, and what it looks like.

Two numbers for one fact is not a reporting bug. It is a modelling gap, and it gets wider with every new report.

## From raw tables to a gold star schema

The fix is the [medallion architecture](gl:medallion-architecture) from [lesson 10.1](lesson:10.1), applied for real:

- **Bronze**: the mirrored and copied tables, exactly as the sources sent them.
- **Silver**: cleaned and conformed. One row per order line, cancellations flagged, VAT split out, customer IDs matched across the shop and the ERP.
- **Gold**: a model built for questions, with the business rules applied once.

Gold is shaped as a **[star schema](gl:star-schema)**. In the middle sits a **[fact table](gl:fact-table)**: one row per business event, holding numbers and keys. Around it sit **[dimension tables](gl:dimension-table)**: the nouns you slice by, such as date, customer, product and region.

Northline's `FactSales` has one row per order line. It holds quantity, net revenue, VAT and discount, plus keys to `DimDate`, `DimCustomer`, `DimProduct` and `DimRegion`. The first design decision is the **grain**, what one row means. ==Fix the grain of the fact table before anything else; most wrong totals come from mixing grains, such as order-level delivery charges repeated on every line.==

Revenue becomes one column computed in one load, under one documented rule. Reports stop writing their own.

## Lakehouse or Warehouse

Fabric gives you two places to keep those tables. Both store Delta tables in [OneLake](gl:onelake), and both are queried by the same SQL engine.

| | **[Fabric Lakehouse](gl:fabric-lakehouse)** | **[Fabric Warehouse](gl:fabric-warehouse)** |
|---|---|---|
| You write with | [Apache Spark](gl:apache-spark): Python, Spark SQL, Scala or R in [notebooks](gl:notebook), plus pipelines and dataflows | T-SQL: `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `MERGE`, `CREATE TABLE AS SELECT`, `COPY INTO`, stored procedures |
| T-SQL access | The **[SQL analytics endpoint](gl:sql-analytics-endpoint)**: read-only. Views, functions and security, but no inserts, updates or deletes | Full read and write T-SQL |
| Transactions | Per table | Multi-table: `BEGIN TRAN` across the fact and its dimensions |
| Data | Tables and files: JSON, CSV, images, anything | Structured tables only |
| Natural owner | Data engineers | SQL developers and analysts |

==The choice is mostly about the team's language, not the data: both keep the same Delta tables in OneLake, and either can read the other's.== A warehouse query can join a lakehouse table by its three-part name without copying it.

So Northline uses both. Kofi's notebooks turn messy bronze into silver in a lakehouse. That suits Spark: clickstream files from Event Hubs Capture, fuzzy customer matching, and volumes that grow every sale. Tom's stored procedures load the gold star into a warehouse. Each night's load of `FactSales` and its dimensions commits as one transaction, so a report never sees new facts pointing at missing customers.

**SQL database in Fabric**, generally available since November 2025, is a third option, but for a different job: it is Azure SQL Database for operational apps, with automatic mirroring for analytics. It is not where a star schema belongs.

## Slowly changing dimensions

In June, customer 48213 moved from the Gold loyalty tier to Platinum. What happens to her January orders?

A **[slowly changing dimension](gl:slowly-changing-dimension)** is the decision about that:

- **Type 1, overwrite.** Update the row in place. Her January orders now count as Platinum. History is rewritten, and nobody can tell. Right for corrections, such as a misspelt name.
- **Type 2, add a row.** Close the old row with an end date and insert a new one with its own surrogate key, valid from June. Facts keep the key that was current when the sale happened, so January stays Gold.

Ask "how much did Platinum members spend last year?" and only type 2 gives an honest answer. Tom uses type 2 for loyalty tier and region and type 1 for everything else. Type 2 costs more rows, a surrogate key on every dimension and careful `MERGE` logic, so choose its columns deliberately.

## In practice

- **Workspace**: one lakehouse for bronze and silver, and one warehouse, `wh_gold`, for the star schema. Reports and the shared model that comes in [lesson 10.6](lesson:10.6) read gold only.
- **Orchestration**: a nightly [data pipeline](gl:data-pipeline) runs Kofi's notebooks, then Tom's load procedures, then refreshes the reports.
- **Revenue**: defined in writing as net of VAT, after cancellations and before refunds. It is one column in `FactSales`, owned by finance.

**What it costs.** Everything draws on the [Fabric capacity](gl:fabric-capacity): Spark sessions while they run, warehouse queries and loads, and OneLake storage per GB. A notebook session left open keeps drawing capacity until it times out, which is how one analyst can [throttle](gl:smoothing-and-throttling) everyone.

**What breaks.**

- **Keys that are not enforced.** Warehouse primary and foreign keys are declared `NOT ENFORCED`. A duplicate customer row silently doubles that customer's revenue. Test for duplicates in every load.
- **Mixed grains**, as above.
- **Writing through the SQL analytics endpoint.** It is read-only. Teams that only know T-SQL hit this on day one; put their tables in a warehouse.
- **Concurrent loads.** The warehouse locks whole tables on writes, so two `MERGE` jobs on one table conflict and one fails. Run loads in sequence.
- **Logic drifting back into reports.** A report that redefines revenue in its own measure restarts the board-meeting argument.

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | In Fabric |
|---|---|
| A Kimball data warehouse feeding an SSAS cube | A gold star schema feeding a shared semantic model |
| A clustered columnstore index | Delta Parquet: columnar by default |
| An indexed view, or a view over staging tables | Views on the SQL analytics endpoint, computed at query time |
| An SSIS package that materialises reporting tables | Notebooks or stored procedures that build gold tables |
| The SSIS Slowly Changing Dimension wizard | A `MERGE` in a stored procedure or notebook |
| A read-only reporting replica | The SQL analytics endpoint |

Views are fine for silver experiments. Gold should be real tables, because every report reads them all day.

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> In a modelling review, ask:
>
> 1. **What is the grain of each fact table, in one sentence?** If nobody can say it, the totals are already wrong.
> 2. **Where is revenue defined, and who owns that definition?** One column, one owner, or two numbers at the next board meeting.
> 3. **Which tool will the people maintaining this actually use, Spark or T-SQL?** Choose Lakehouse or Warehouse for the team, not the hype.
> 4. **Which dimension attributes need history, type 2, and which can be overwritten, type 1?** Decide per column, before the first load.
> 5. **How do we catch duplicate keys and failed loads?** The warehouse will not enforce keys for you.

## Key takeaways

- Two numbers for one fact is a modelling gap. Build gold once, as a star schema, and point every report at it.
- A star schema is a fact table at a stated grain, surrounded by dimension tables. Fix the grain first.
- A Lakehouse is written with Spark and read with T-SQL through a read-only SQL analytics endpoint. A Warehouse is full read-write T-SQL with multi-table transactions. Both store Delta tables in OneLake.
- Choose Spark or T-SQL by your team's skills; many teams use a lakehouse for bronze and silver and a warehouse for gold.
- Slowly changing dimensions: type 1 overwrites history, type 2 keeps it with a new row. Use type 2 only where history matters.
