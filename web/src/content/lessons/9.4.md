## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] "Everyone certified by December"
> The CTO sets a goal: every engineer holds an Azure certification by the end of the year, and there is a budget for it. Within a week, Dev has passed AZ-900 by memorising questions from a website. Ana wants to go straight for the architect exam. Two senior engineers ask what the point is.
>
> Meanwhile, Azure itself keeps moving. Services are renamed, retired and launched every month, as Module 8 showed. Priya wants certifications that reflect real skill, and a team that stays current after the exams are over.

## How Microsoft certification works

Microsoft's **[role-based certifications](gl:role-based-certification)** are organised by job and level:

| Level | What it proves | Examples | Expires? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fundamentals** | Vocabulary and concepts | [AZ-900](gl:az-900), AI-900 | No |
| **Associate** | Doing the job day to day | [AZ-104](gl:az-104) (administrator), AZ-204 (developer), AI-102 (AI engineer), AZ-500 (security) | Yearly renewal |
| **Expert** | Designing and leading | [AZ-305](gl:az-305) (solutions architect), [AZ-400](gl:az-400) (DevOps engineer) | Yearly renewal |
| **Specialty** | A narrow product area | Azure Virtual Desktop | Yearly renewal |

Associate and expert exams are mostly scenario questions and case studies, much like the module tests in this course. You can open Microsoft Learn during the exam, so they test judgement more than recall. An exam costs roughly $165 in the US, and the price varies by country. **[Certification renewal](gl:certification-renewal)** is a free, unproctored online assessment, open in the six months before expiry. It covers what changed in the past year, which is the point of it.

> [!IMPORTANT] Check the exam page
> Exams are revised, replaced and retired regularly, and the AI exams change fastest. Each exam's *skills measured* page on Microsoft Learn is the real syllabus. Read it, and any retirement notice, before you book.

## Studying so it sticks

- **Build, don't memorise.** Each learner gets a sandbox subscription through vending ([lesson 9.2](lesson:9.2)) with a small budget and an auto-delete policy. Everything studied gets deployed, broken and fixed there.
- **Use [Microsoft Learn](gl:microsoft-learn)**: free learning paths, hands-on sandboxes, the free official practice assessment for each exam, and an exam sandbox to try the interface.
- **Space it out.** Short, regular sessions beat a weekend cram. This course's quizzes and module tests are built for it: revisit a module's test a month later.
- **Teach it.** A 20-minute lunchtime talk on what you learnt is the best test of whether you understand it.
- **Never use exam dumps.** They break the exam agreement, can get a certification revoked, and leave you with a badge but no skill.

**[Applied Skills](gl:applied-skills)** credentials complement exams. They are short, lab-based assessments of one scenario, such as securing storage or deploying containers. You pass by doing the task in a live environment, which is good evidence of hands-on ability.

## Keeping up with Azure

Certification is a snapshot. Staying current is a habit:

- **Azure Updates** and the retirement notices from Service Health and Advisor show what is new and what is going away, including model retirements ([lesson 8.8](lesson:8.8)).
- **The Azure Architecture Center** and the Well-Architected guides are updated as services change ([lesson 9.1](lesson:9.1)).
- **Build and Ignite session recordings** for the big shifts, once or twice a year.
- **Your own production**: incidents, reviews and cost reports teach more than any course. Write them up.
- **A team ritual**: 30 minutes a month on "what changed that affects us", with a rotating presenter and a short list of actions.

## In practice

Priya rewrites the goal as "the right certification for each role, and a habit that outlasts it":

- **Each engineer picks a path** with their manager, from the table above. Two seniors go for AZ-305, most developers AZ-204, and the platform team AZ-104 then AZ-700.
- **Four hours a week of study time** during working hours, with sandbox subscriptions capped at $50 a month.
- **Exam fees are paid for passes and fails alike**, so nobody feels pushed towards dumps. Renewal dates go in the team calendar.
- **Dev retakes his study the right way** and adds an Applied Skills credential for Container Apps.
- **The monthly "what changed" session** starts in October. Its first topic is a model retirement notice.

**What breaks.**

- **Certification as a KPI**, which drives people to exam dumps.
- **Badges without building**: people who pass but cannot deploy.
- **Everyone taking the same exam** whatever their role.
- **Lapsed renewals**, and knowledge a year out of date.
- **Learning only from courses**, never from your own incidents.

| You already know | In Azure learning |
|---|---|
| MCSD or MCSA from the .NET years | Role-based associate and expert certifications, renewed yearly |
| A Pluralsight course | A Microsoft Learn learning path with sandboxes |
| A weekend side project | A sandbox subscription with a budget |
| Release notes for a framework you depend on | Azure Updates and retirement notices |
| A conference talk | A lunchtime session for the team |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> For a learning plan:
>
> 1. **Which certification matches each person's role, and why?**
> 2. **Where do people build what they study?** Is it budget-capped?
> 3. **How much study time is protected each week?**
> 4. **How will we hear about retirements and changes that affect us?**
> 5. **What did our last incident teach us, and who wrote it up?**

## Key takeaways

- Microsoft certifications come in fundamentals, associate, expert and specialty levels. Associate and expert certifications renew yearly through a free online assessment.
- Choose by role: AZ-104 then AZ-305 for leads and architects, AZ-204 for developers, AZ-104 then AZ-700 or AZ-500 for platform engineers, and AI-102 for AI engineers.
- The skills-measured page is the real syllabus, and exams change, so check it before booking.
- Build in a sandbox, use Learn and its practice assessments, space out study, teach it, and never use dumps. Applied Skills prove hands-on ability.
- Stay current with Azure Updates, retirement notices, the Architecture Center, a monthly team session and your own incidents.
