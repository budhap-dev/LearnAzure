## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] The Monday-morning invoice
> Three weeks into the Azure pilot, Priya's finance partner forwards the first invoice with one word: "Explain?" It is four times the estimate. The trail is short. A developer created an Azure SQL database on a Business Critical tier "just to test performance" and left it. A load-test virtual machine ran all weekend. And a script that copied the 40 GB test database from UK South to West Europe for a failover rehearsal ran nine times because of a retry bug - nine lots of outbound data transfer.
>
> Nobody broke an on-premises rule. On-premises, an idle server costs nothing extra. In the cloud, the meter never stops.

[Lesson 1.1](lesson:1.1) said cloud cost is a behaviour problem. This lesson is the behaviour: what the meter counts, which dials move it, and the tools and habits that stop the Monday-morning invoice.

## What the meter counts

[Pay-as-you-go](gl:pay-as-you-go) sounds simple until you see how many meters a small web workload has:

| Resource | What you are billed for | The surprise |
|---|---|---|
| **Compute** (VMs, App Service) | Per second or hour, by size and tier, while it exists | A VM *stopped* inside the OS still bills; it must be *deallocated* |
| **Storage** | Per GB per month, plus per-transaction charges | Millions of tiny blob reads cost more than the bytes |
| **Database** | Per hour by vCore or DTU; per second on serverless | Premium tiers cost many times the basic ones; nothing warns you |
| **Data transfer** | Outbound to the internet or another region - [egress](gl:egress). Inbound is free | Cross-region copies, uncached images |
| **Per-request services** | Functions consumption bills per execution and per GB-second | A runaway loop is a bill, not a crash |
| **Hidden items** | Public IPs, log ingestion, backups, snapshots | Small each, a steady drip together |

==In Azure you pay for existence, not usefulness: a provisioned resource that is idle costs the same as one doing work.==

Four factors move every price:

1. **Region** - the same VM size can differ by 10-30% between regions ([lesson 1.3](lesson:1.3)).
2. **Tier or SKU** - Basic, Standard, Premium; General Purpose or Business Critical. The biggest single multiplier.
3. **Redundancy** - more copies across zones or regions cost more.
4. **Running time** - eight hours a day, five days a week is roughly a quarter of always-on.

## Estimating before you build

Two free tools live on the Azure website. The **[Azure pricing calculator](gl:azure-pricing-calculator)** turns a design - services, region, tier, hours - into a monthly estimate you can save and share. A design that has not been priced is not finished. The **[TCO calculator](gl:tco-calculator)** compares on-premises spend (hardware, licences, power, people) with Azure over several years; it argues the migration, not the monthly bill. Both are estimates. The real bill depends on what actually runs.

## What is free

The **[Azure free account](gl:free-account)** gives a new sign-up a credit for the first 30 days, twelve months of limited amounts of popular services, and a set of **always-free** services within monthly limits. Separately, several services have permanent free tiers that suit learning and side projects:

| Service | Free offer (amounts change - check the current page) |
|---|---|
| ![Static Web Apps](az:static-web-apps) Static Web Apps | A Free plan for a React front end with custom domains |
| ![App Service](az:app-service) App Service | The F1 plan: shared compute, limited minutes per day |
| ![Functions](az:function-apps) Azure Functions | A monthly grant of executions on the consumption plan |
| ![Azure SQL](az:azure-sql) Azure SQL Database | A small serverless database with a monthly vCore allowance |

Free tiers carry no service-level agreement, which [lesson 1.7](lesson:1.7) explains. They are for learning, not customers.

## The cost-saving levers

| Lever | What it does | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Switch off / auto-shutdown** | Deallocate VMs, pause serverless databases when idle | Dev, test, demo |
| **[Right-sizing](gl:right-sizing)** | Drop to the size or tier that matches real utilisation | Anything under 20% CPU for weeks |
| **[Reservations](gl:reservation)** | Commit to a size for one or three years; 30-70% off | Steady production compute and databases |
| **[Savings plans](gl:savings-plan)** | Commit to an hourly compute spend, not a size | Steady spend whose shape may change |
| **[Spot virtual machines](gl:spot-virtual-machine)** | Spare capacity at up to 90% off, evicted at short notice | Batch jobs, CI agents |
| **[Azure Hybrid Benefit](gl:azure-hybrid-benefit)** | Apply existing Windows Server and SQL Server licences | Anyone with Software Assurance |
| **Serverless / consumption** | Pay per execution or second of real use | Spiky or low traffic |
| **Dev/Test pricing** | Discounted rates on non-production subscriptions | Visual Studio subscribers |

- **Cost analysis** breaks the bill down by service, resource group, location or tag, with a forecast. Priya's team found the Business Critical database here in two clicks.
- A **[budget](gl:budget)** is a monthly amount on a subscription or resource group. It does not stop spending; it fires **[cost alerts](gl:cost-alert)** at thresholds you choose, by email or to an action that can, say, shut down a resource group.
- **Tags** (`team`, `environment`, `cost-centre`) make the bill attributable, which enables **showback** (each team sees its spend) and **[chargeback](gl:chargeback)** (finance bills them). [Lesson 1.4](lesson:1.4) shows how policy makes a tag mandatory.
- **[Azure Advisor](gl:azure-advisor)** watches utilisation and names specific fixes: this VM is idle, this database would be cheaper reserved, this disk is unattached.

## In practice

Three habits do most of the work: a **budget with alerts on every subscription** on the day it is created, including the sandbox; **tags enforced by policy**, so nothing untagged deploys; and a **weekly fifteen-minute cost review** by whoever owns the bill, reading cost analysis and Advisor.

> [!WARNING] What a budget does not do
> A 100% alert arrives after the money is spent. Prevention is auto-shutdown schedules, modest default tiers in templates, and a policy that blocks premium SKUs outside production.

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | The Azure equivalent |
|---|---|
| A mobile data plan metered per gigabyte | Pay-as-you-go: every meter counts, the bill arrives after |
| An annual gym membership - cheaper per visit if you go | A reservation: commit for a year, save a lot, waste it if you stop |
| SQL Server licences you already pay for | Azure Hybrid Benefit applies them to Azure SQL and Windows VMs |
| Leaving `npm run dev` running overnight | Harmless on a laptop; on a cloud box the meter runs until Monday |
| A bug that retries a request nine times | Nine lots of egress and nine lots of executions |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> Before anything reaches production, ask:
>
> 1. **What is the monthly estimate, and where is the calculator link?** No estimate, no approval.
> 2. **Which tier, and why not the one below it?** Defaults drift to premium.
> 3. **What switches off at night?** Every non-production resource needs an answer.
> 4. **Which tags are on it, and who gets the alert?** Cost without an owner is nobody's problem.
> 5. **What leaves the region, and how often?** Egress is the charge people forget.

## Key takeaways

- You pay for compute time, storage volume and transactions, database capacity, outbound data and a drip of small items - whether or not anything uses them.
- Region, tier, redundancy and running time are the four dials on every price.
- The pricing calculator estimates a design; the TCO calculator argues a migration; Cost Management tracks reality.
- Reservations and savings plans reward steady workloads; switching off, right-sizing and serverless reward variable ones.
- Budgets alert, they do not block. Tags, policy and a weekly review keep the bill honest.
