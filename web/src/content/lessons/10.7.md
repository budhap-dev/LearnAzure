## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] Where is Helen's data?
> Helen Marsh, a loyalty member since 2019, emails Northline: close my account and erase everything you hold about me. UK GDPR gives Northline one month to answer.
>
> Ana removes her from the orders database in an afternoon. Then the questions start. Her orders also sit in the mirrored copy in OneLake and in the bronze lakehouse. Her signed-in clicks sit in two years of clickstream Parquet files. Her old loyalty tiers sit as history rows in the gold customer dimension. And a marketing analyst exported "top 1,000 loyalty members" to a CSV on his OneDrive last spring.
>
> Priya asks Tom a simple question: where is all of Helen's data? He cannot answer it. Nobody can.

Every lesson in this module has made a copy. This one is about knowing where the copies are and who answers for them.

## What Purview gives a data platform

**[Microsoft Purview](gl:microsoft-purview)** is Microsoft's family of governance, data security and compliance tools, run from one Microsoft Purview portal. Three parts matter to a data team:

| Part | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **[Data Map](gl:data-map)** | What data do we hold, where, and what is in it? It scans Azure SQL, the lake, Fabric, on-premises SQL Server and other clouds. |
| **[Unified Catalog](gl:unified-catalog)** | What does it mean, who owns it, can I trust it? Glossary terms, governance domains, data products and data quality. |
| **Information Protection and DLP** | How must it be handled, and where may it go? Labels and policies across Microsoft 365 and Fabric. |

Fabric already includes a lot: the **OneLake catalog** finds items, groups them into domains, marks trusted ones as certified and shows lineage, all within the Fabric licence. Purview adds the estate beyond Fabric, plus labels, DLP and audit.

==Purview holds metadata, never the data itself: it tells you which tables hold email addresses, not which rows are Helen's.==

## Find it: classification and labels

A **scan** reads a source on a schedule and records its schemas and columns in the Data Map. The Daventry SQL Server is reached through a self-hosted [integration runtime](gl:integration-runtime).

While it scans, the Data Map samples values and applies **[data classification](gl:data-classification)**. There are built-in classifications, such as a person's name, a credit card number or a passport number. You can add custom ones as a regular expression, such as Northline's loyalty number.

A **[sensitivity label](gl:sensitivity-label)** is different. Classification describes what a column contains; a label says how an item must be handled, such as "Confidential - Customer data". They are the same Purview Information Protection labels staff already put on Word files. In Fabric, a label on the silver lakehouse flows down to the semantic models and reports built from it. It survives export to Excel, PowerPoint and PDF, but not to CSV, which is how the analyst's file got out unmarked.

**[Data loss prevention](gl:data-loss-prevention)** (DLP) policies look for sensitive content where it lands. In Fabric they check lakehouses, warehouses and semantic models and alert the owner; restricting access is still in preview for some item types. DLP for OneDrive catches a CSV of customer email addresses when it is saved or shared.

## Trace it and own it: lineage and stewards

**[Data lineage](gl:data-lineage)** is the map of how data flows from source to report. Finance asks where "net revenue" in the board pack comes from. Fabric's lineage view answers item by item, from the mirrored orders database through Kofi's cleaning notebook and Tom's gold load procedure, which applies the revenue rule, to the semantic model and the report. Purview joins up lineage across Data Factory, Synapse and Databricks too. Some sources give column-level lineage; most Fabric items give item-level only.

The Unified Catalog adds meaning: the glossary term "Net revenue" has one definition, linked to the gold measure that calculates it. Each domain also names two people. The **owner** is accountable for the data; the finance director owns sales. The **[data steward](gl:data-steward)** does the daily work: definitions, classifications, quality rules and access requests. Tom stewards sales and customer data.

==A dataset with no named owner is a dataset nobody deletes from when the erasure request arrives.==

## In practice

Tom's erasure runbook, now a page in the catalog:

1. **Find.** The catalog lists 23 tables in five sources with columns classified as customer identifiers. Query each for her customer ID.
2. **Delete at the source.** Remove her loyalty and marketing data from Azure SQL; mirroring carries the deletes into OneLake. Tax law keeps her invoices for six years, and the reply says so. Erasure is not absolute.
3. **Silver and gold.** Delete her rows from the Delta tables, including the old history rows in the customer dimension. A Delta `DELETE` keeps the old files for time travel, so run `VACUUM` afterwards. With the default seven-day retention, she is gone from storage a week later.
4. **Bronze.** Rewriting two years of append-only Parquet for one customer is slow and risky. The lasting fix is to **pseudonymise at ingestion**: the telemetry API from [lesson 5.4](lesson:5.4) now sends a token, not the customer ID, so Capture only ever writes tokens, and a single table maps tokens to customers. Delete her row from that table and nothing links those clicks to her any more.
5. **Reports and files.** The Direct Lake model picks up the deletes from gold; any Import-mode model that holds customer columns is refreshed. A DLP policy finds the CSV, and the analyst deletes it.

**What it costs.** Unified Catalog is pay-as-you-go, per governed asset per day: a table linked to a data product or glossary term. Northline pays for the 40 gold tables it curates, not the 2,000 it scanned; scanning carries no separate charge. Data-quality rules add processing units. Labels and DLP come with Microsoft 365 licences: E3 for manual labels, an E5-level licence for automatic labelling and for DLP in Fabric. The biggest cost is a steward's time every week.

**What breaks.**

- **Personal data in bronze "because it is raw".** Every copy you keep is a copy you must erase.
- **CSV exports.** The label does not go with them.
- **Treating lineage as complete.** Item-level lineage shows that a notebook touches a table, not which columns it changes.

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | In Purview and Fabric |
|---|---|
| SQL Server's `ADD SENSITIVITY CLASSIFICATION`, with an information type and a label | Classification and sensitivity labels: the same two ideas, across every source |
| The data dictionary page in the team wiki | Unified Catalog glossary terms, kept next to the data |
| "View Dependencies" in SSMS | Lineage, across tools rather than within one database |
| A `DELETE` that still lives in the log and the backups | A Delta `DELETE`, which lives on in old files until `VACUUM` |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> Before a new source lands in the lake, ask:
>
> 1. **Does bronze need its personal data unmasked?** If not, pseudonymise at ingestion, because every raw copy is a copy to erase.
> 2. **Who owns it and who stewards it?** Two names in the catalog before the first load, or nobody answers the next request.
> 3. **What sensitivity label does it get?** A label on the lakehouse reaches every report built from it.
> 4. **How would we erase one person from it within a month?** If the answer is "rewrite everything", change the design while it is cheap.
> 5. **Which exports leave Fabric, and in what format?** A CSV leaves without its label.

## Key takeaways

- The Data Map scans and classifies, the Unified Catalog adds meaning and ownership, and labels and DLP control handling.
- Purview holds metadata only. It shows which tables hold personal data; your own queries find one person's rows.
- Classification says what a column contains; a sensitivity label says how to handle it, flows downstream in Fabric and is lost in CSV exports.
- Lineage answers "where did this number come from?", item by item in Fabric. Treat it as a map, not a proof.
- Every dataset needs an accountable owner and a steward who does the work.
- Erasure in a lake means deleting from silver and gold, vacuuming Delta history, and pseudonymising before bronze.
