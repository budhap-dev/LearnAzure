## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] Twenty-three subscriptions later
> Eighteen months after the first workload, Northline has 23 subscriptions, created by whoever asked. There are three separate hub networks. The loyalty and wholesale spokes use overlapping IP ranges, so they cannot be peered. An auditor found a storage account open to the internet, and nobody knows who owns it. Finance cannot say what AI costs per team. And a new team has waited six weeks for a subscription, raised through an IT ticket.
>
> The CTO asks Priya to "sort out the cloud platform". It is the first thing in this course that is about the whole organisation rather than one workload.

## The Cloud Adoption Framework

The **[Cloud Adoption Framework](gl:cloud-adoption-framework)** (CAF) is Microsoft's guidance for adopting Azure as an organisation. Its methodologies cover strategy, planning, getting ready, adopting (migrating and building), and then governing, securing and managing what you run. The **Ready** step is where landing zones come from, and Govern is where this lesson's policies and FinOps practice come from. If the Well-Architected Framework is about one workload, CAF is about all of them.

## Azure landing zones

An **[Azure landing zone](gl:azure-landing-zone)** is the pre-built environment that workloads land in: subscriptions already organised, networked, secured, monitored and governed. It has two parts:

- **The platform landing zone**: shared services owned by a platform team. That means the hub network with its firewall and DNS, identity, central logging and the policies that apply to everyone.
- **Application landing zones**: one or more subscriptions per workload, such as `loyalty-prod`. Each is a spoke peered to the hub, inheriting policy from above. The workload team owns what is inside.

The reference design names eight **design areas**, and each is a decision Priya has to make once for everyone:

| Design area | Northline's decision |
|---|---|
| Billing and tenant | One Entra tenant, one billing agreement |
| Identity and access | Entra groups mapped to roles at management-group scope; PIM for Owner ([lesson 4.5](lesson:4.5)) |
| Resource organisation | The hierarchy above; one subscription per workload per environment |
| Network topology | [Hub-and-spoke](gl:hub-and-spoke), with a documented IP plan: a /16 per region, a /22 per spoke |
| Security | Defender for Cloud on every subscription, logs to Sentinel ([lesson 4.7](lesson:4.7)) |
| Management | Central Log Analytics for platform logs; workload telemetry stays with the workload |
| Governance | Azure Policy initiatives at management-group scope; tags and budgets |
| Platform automation and DevOps | Everything above is code in a repository, deployed by a pipeline |

## Governance with Azure Policy

[Azure Policy](gl:azure-policy) ([lesson 1.4](lesson:1.4)) is how rules become automatic. Definitions are grouped into a **[policy initiative](gl:policy-initiative)**, which is assigned at a management group so every subscription beneath inherits it. What happens to a non-compliant resource depends on the **[policy effect](gl:policy-effect)**:

| Effect | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit** | Reports non-compliance, blocks nothing | Storage accounts without private endpoints |
| **Deny** | Blocks the create or update | Public network access on storage; regions outside the EU |
| **Modify / Append** | Changes the request as it happens | Copy the `cost-centre` tag from the subscription |
| **DeployIfNotExists** | Deploys a missing companion resource | Diagnostic settings to Log Analytics |

The rollout rule: ==Audit first, fix what it finds, then Deny.== Denying on day one blocks teams without warning, and blocked teams create shadow subscriptions. Exemptions exist for real exceptions, with an owner and an expiry date. Keep policy as code in a repository, deployed through the pipeline, and never clicked into the portal.

## Subscription vending and the platform team

**[Subscription vending](gl:subscription-vending)** replaces the six-week ticket with a pipeline. A team opens a pull request with a short file: name, owner, budget, environment and requested address range. The pipeline creates the subscription under the right management group, peers its spoke to the hub, assigns roles to the team's groups, sets the budget and applies tags. Every new subscription starts compliant.

A **[platform team](gl:platform-team)** owns the landing zone as a product. Its customers are the workload teams, and its success is measured by how quickly they get a safe place to build. ==The platform team's product is a subscription that is secure, connected and governed from its first minute, delivered in an hour rather than six weeks.== A platform team that acts as a gatekeeper will be routed around.

## FinOps and visibility

**[FinOps](gl:finops)** is the practice of making cloud cost a shared, continuous responsibility of engineering, finance and product. It works in three phases: inform (who spends what), optimise (rightsizing, commitments, waste) and operate (budgets, reviews, accountability). On Azure that means:

- **Required tags** such as `cost-centre`, `owner` and `app`, enforced by policy, so costs can be allocated.
- **Showback, then [chargeback](gl:chargeback)**, built from Cost Management exports.
- **Budgets per subscription**, with anomaly alerts.
- **Commitments bought centrally**: [reservations](gl:reservation) and savings plans ([lesson 1.6](lesson:1.6)).

For "what exists?", **[Azure Resource Graph](gl:azure-resource-graph)** answers queries across every subscription in seconds. The auditor's question, "every storage account with public network access", is one query rather than 23 portal sessions.

## In practice

- **Built from the Azure landing zone accelerator** in Terraform, adapted and kept in the platform repository.
- **The 23 subscriptions** are moved under the new management groups. Every policy runs in Audit for 30 days, the findings are fixed, and then the security-critical policies switch to Deny.
- **Networking**: one hub per region, with the new IP plan. Wholesale is re-addressed during its next release so it can peer.
- **Vending** takes under an hour. The platform team of three reviews the pull request, not a ticket.
- **Cost**: tags are enforced, AI and data costs are shown back per team monthly, and chargeback starts next year.

**What it costs.** Management groups, Policy and Resource Graph are free. The hub is not: Azure Firewall Standard is roughly $900 a month before traffic, and DDoS Network Protection nearly $3,000 a month. Both are worth deciding on deliberately, not by default.

**What breaks.**

- **A landing zone treated as a one-off project** with no team to run it afterwards.
- **Deny everything on day one**, which leads to shadow subscriptions.
- **Policies clicked into the portal**, with no history and no review.
- **No IP address plan**, so spokes overlap and cannot peer.
- **A sandbox peered to the corporate network.**

| A CMDB spreadsheet | Azure Resource Graph |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> For the platform, and for every new workload:
>
> 1. **Which management group does this subscription sit under, and which initiatives does it inherit?**
> 2. **How long does a new team wait for a compliant subscription?**
> 3. **Is every policy in code, and was it audited before it denied?**
> 4. **Where is the IP address plan, and who allocates from it?**
> 5. **Can we show each team its own cloud cost this month?**

## Key takeaways

- The Cloud Adoption Framework guides adoption across the organisation. Landing zones come from its Ready step, and policy and FinOps from Govern.
- An Azure landing zone has a platform part (hub, identity, logging, policy) and application landing zones: a subscription per workload per environment, as spokes.
- Management groups carry policy initiatives and access down to every subscription. Audit first, then Deny, and keep policy as code.
- Subscription vending delivers a compliant, connected subscription in an hour. The platform team runs the landing zone as a product.
- FinOps makes cost a shared job through enforced tags, showback, budgets and central commitments. Azure Resource Graph answers "what exists?" across everything.
