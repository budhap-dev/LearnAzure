## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] "Just send it all to the big model"
> Northline's finance team types in 6,000 supplier invoices a month. Customer service records 2,000 calls a week that nobody ever listens to. Product reviews arrive in five languages, and the warehouse photographs every delivery note.
>
> Dev has a plan for all of it: send every file, recording and review to the flagship model with vision and audio, and ask it what it sees. His demo read an invoice perfectly.
>
> Finance asks what 12,000 pages a month will cost that way, and what happens when it misreads a total. Priya suspects there is a cheaper, more predictable tool for most of this.

## The prebuilt services

**[Azure AI Services](gl:azure-ai-services)** are pretrained models for well-defined tasks, called through a REST API or SDK. You send an input, and you get back structured output with confidence scores. They have no prompts and nothing to train, and they are priced per page, per hour of audio or per character. In Microsoft Foundry they are reached through the same resource and identity as the models ([lesson 8.3](lesson:8.3)).

| Service | What it does | At the shop | Priced per |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Document Intelligence](gl:document-intelligence)** | Reads text, tables and fields from documents; prebuilt invoice, receipt and ID models; custom models from a few samples | Supplier invoices, delivery notes | Page |
| **[Azure AI Vision](gl:azure-ai-vision)** | Describes and tags images, reads text in photos, detects objects | Tagging product photos, reading a label | Image |
| **[Azure AI Speech](gl:azure-ai-speech)** | Speech to text (live and batch), text to speech with natural voices, speech translation | Transcribing service calls | Hour of audio or character |
| **[Azure AI Language](gl:azure-ai-language)** | Sentiment, key phrases, entities, PII detection and redaction, summarisation, custom classification | Redacting and summarising call transcripts | Text record |
| **[Azure AI Translator](gl:azure-ai-translator)** | Text and whole-document translation in 100+ languages, with custom terminology | Reviews and product pages | Character |
| **[Content Understanding](gl:content-understanding)** | Generative extraction from documents, images, audio and video into a schema you define | Varied supplier paperwork | Content processed |

Two more live in the same family. **Content Safety** is covered in [lesson 8.7](lesson:8.7). **Face** detects and compares faces, and recognising individuals is available only to approved customers under Microsoft's responsible AI rules.

## Prebuilt service, custom model, or an LLM?

Every task in the scenario *could* go to a large model with vision or audio. The question is whether it *should*.

| | Task-specific service | Large language model |
|---|---|---|
| Cost at volume | Low and predictable, per page or per hour | Per token; images and audio use many tokens |
| Output | Fixed structure with field names, confidence scores and positions on the page | Whatever the prompt asks for, which may vary between runs |
| Consistency | The same input gives the same output | Can vary, and can invent a plausible value |
| Flexibility | Only the task it was built for | Handles odd layouts and questions nobody planned for |
| Proof | Confidence per field, bounding boxes for review | Needs grounding and evaluation |

==Use a task-specific service when the task is well defined and the volume is high. Use a generative model when the input varies or the output needs judgement.== Content Understanding sits in between: it uses generative models underneath, but returns fields to your schema with confidence scores and where each value was found.

When no prebuilt model fits, **train a custom one** on your own samples before reaching for an LLM. A custom Document Intelligence model learns a supplier's delivery-note layout from a handful of labelled examples, and a custom Language classifier learns your complaint categories.

## Confidence and review

Every field comes back with a **confidence** score between 0 and 1. That is what makes these services safe to automate. Set a threshold per field. Above it, the value flows straight through. Below it, a person checks it on a review screen that highlights the field on the original page. The team stops typing and starts checking, and the threshold moves as the numbers prove themselves.

Priya's decisions, one idea at a time:

- **Invoices**: Document Intelligence's prebuilt invoice model, about $10 per thousand pages, so roughly $120 a month for 12,000 pages. Fields above 0.9 confidence post automatically. After a month, 80% of invoices need no human touch, and the three people handle exceptions and supplier queries instead.
- **Delivery notes**: a custom Document Intelligence model trained on labelled examples from the five biggest suppliers. Everything else goes to Content Understanding.
- **Calls**: batch transcription with Speech, which is cheaper than live and fine a day later. Language then redacts card numbers and addresses before anything is stored, summarises each call and tags its sentiment, so complaint trends show up weekly.
- **Reviews**: Translator into English for the moderation team, and Language sentiment on every review. Themes are summarised by a small LLM once a week, because that task needs judgement and the volume is small.
- **Warehouse**: Document Intelligence runs in a **container** on the warehouse server, so delivery notes are read even when the internet link drops. Connected containers still report usage for billing.

**What breaks.**

- **A flagship model for bulk extraction.** It costs more, gives no field-level confidence, and occasionally invents a plausible total.
- **No threshold**, so either everything goes to review or mistakes post silently.
- **Custom models trained on one supplier's layout**, which then fail on the next.
- **Personal data kept in raw transcripts** that should have been redacted before storage.
- **Assuming every feature exists in every region.** Check availability before choosing a region.

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | In Azure AI Services |
|---|---|
| Tesseract or another OCR library | Vision's Read, or Document Intelligence's read and layout models |
| Regex over text extracted from PDFs | Prebuilt and custom Document Intelligence models |
| Dictation software | Speech to text |
| A sentiment npm package | Language sentiment analysis |
| Google Translate on a web page | Translator, with custom terminology |
| A validation rule that routes bad rows to a queue | Confidence thresholds and a review queue |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> For each AI Services use case:
>
> 1. **Is there a prebuilt model for this?** If not, can a custom one learn it from our samples?
> 2. **What confidence threshold posts automatically, and who reviews the rest?**
> 3. **What does it cost per page, hour or character, at next year's volume?**
> 4. **Where does personal data appear, and is it redacted before storage?**
> 5. **Does this need judgement, or just extraction?** Only the first needs an LLM.

## Key takeaways

- Azure AI Services are pretrained models for defined tasks: documents, vision, speech, language and translation. They return structured output with confidence scores and are priced per page, hour or character.
- Use them when the task is defined and the volume is high; use an LLM when the input varies or needs judgement. Content Understanding sits between the two.
- Train a custom model on your own samples before reaching for a large model.
- Confidence thresholds turn extraction into automation: confident fields flow through, and the rest go to a person.
- Redact personal data early, check regional availability, and use containers where the internet cannot be relied on.
