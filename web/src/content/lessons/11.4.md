## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] Out for delivery, at 3 a.m.
> Customers want two things from the app: "your order is out for delivery" and "the boots you wanted are back in stock". Marketing wants a third: a spring promotion pushed to all 400,000 installs.
>
> Last year Leo's prototype called Firebase straight from the Node API. One night a retry loop ran with an empty audience filter, and every Android user got "Your order is out for delivery" at 3 a.m. Twice. Uninstalls tripled that week, and nobody could say who had been sent what.
>
> Priya asks Ana and Leo for one way to send pushes that reaches iPhones and Androids, targets the right people, respects consent and night-time, and cannot broadcast by accident.

## How a push reaches a phone

Your backend never talks to a phone. Only two services can: **[Apple Push Notification service](gl:apple-push-notification-service)** (APNs) for iOS and **[Firebase Cloud Messaging](gl:firebase-cloud-messaging)** (FCM) for Android.

The journey has five steps:

1. The app asks the user for permission. On iOS that is always a prompt. On Android 13 and later, notifications are **off by default for new installs** until the user grants the runtime permission.
2. The operating system gives the app a **[device token](gl:device-token)**: an address for this app on this phone. It changes on reinstall, restore and sometimes for no visible reason.
3. The app sends the token to your API on every launch.
4. Your backend stores it, with who the user is and what they want.
5. To send, your backend hands a payload and an audience to APNs and FCM, which deliver it.

**[Azure Notification Hubs](gl:azure-notification-hubs)** does steps 4 and 5 at scale. It stores every device, holds your APNs and FCM credentials, and turns one send into thousands of platform calls.

Credentials are where teams get stuck:

- **APNs:** use **token-based authentication**: a `.p8` key from the Apple Developer portal, with its Key ID, Team ID and bundle ID. Unlike the old certificates, it does not expire every year. The hub's endpoint, sandbox or production, must match how the app was signed, or every send fails.
- **FCM:** Google shut down the legacy FCM API from July 2024. Only **FCM v1** works, authenticated with a Google service account (project ID, client email and private key). Notification Hubs treats FCM v1 as a separate platform, so devices must be registered as FCM v1 and payloads use the v1 shape. A hub still holding legacy registrations sends nothing to Android.

## Installations, tags and templates

A **[device installation](gl:device-installation)** is the hub's record of one app on one phone: an installation ID the app generates and keeps, the platform, the device token, a list of tags and a set of templates. Creating it is a `PUT` that is safe to repeat; the hub deletes records whose tokens APNs or FCM report as dead.

Tags are plain strings, up to 60 per installation. Northline's scheme:

- `user:8812`: every device of customer 8812.
- `stock:SKU-4471`: added when the customer taps "notify me".
- `region:uk` or `region:in`, for time zone and campaigns.
- `optin:marketing`: only once the customer has said yes in the app.

A send names its audience with a **[tag expression](gl:tag-expression)**, a Boolean filter with `&&`, `||`, `!` and brackets. A mixed expression can use at most six tags.

| Notification | Kind | Tag expression |
|---|---|---|
| Out for delivery | Transactional | `user:8812` |
| Back in stock | Transactional, asked for | `stock:SKU-4471` |
| Spring promotion, UK | Marketing | `optin:marketing && region:uk` |

==Tags are permissions as much as addresses, so only your backend should ever set them.== If the app registers itself with a Listen key, a tampered app can add `user:9000` and read someone else's delivery updates. Leo's app sends its token to the API with its sign-in token from [lesson 11.3](lesson:11.3); the API decides the tags.

A **[notification template](gl:notification-template)** lets each installation say how its payload should look. The iOS template is `{"aps":{"alert":{"title":"$(title)","body":"$(body)"}}}`, the Android one is the FCM v1 equivalent, and the backend sends one set of properties to both. A phone set to Hindi can register a template that reads `$(body_hi)` instead of `$(body)`, so customers in India who prefer Hindi get the same send in Hindi. Old app versions keep the template they registered, which helps the [API versioning](gl:api-versioning) story from [lesson 11.1](lesson:11.1).

A **[silent notification](gl:silent-notification)** carries data and no alert, to wake the app and refresh its cached orders. Apple throttles them, suggests no more than two or three an hour, and drops them if the user force-quit the app. Treat them as a hint, never as delivery.

## In practice

**The tier.** Prices are approximate pay-as-you-go list prices and vary by region:

| Tier | A month | Pushes | Devices |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0, no SLA | 1M | 500 |
| **Basic** | About $10 | 10M | 200,000 |
| **Standard** | About $200 | 10M | 10M |

400,000 installs rules out Basic. Standard also adds **scheduled sends**, per-message telemetry and bulk import. Dev and test get their own Free namespace, so a test script cannot reach a customer.

**Consent and quiet hours.** Apple's review rules allow marketing pushes only after an explicit opt-in in the app, with a way to opt out. The hub does not know anyone's local time. Northline's backend schedules campaigns for 10 a.m. per region, and holds non-urgent transactional pushes between 9 p.m. and 8 a.m.

**The broadcast guard.** A send with no tag expression goes to every device. That was the 3 a.m. bug. ==Every send path must refuse an empty audience, and a campaign must pass a test send to staff devices before it reaches customers.== Campaigns sit behind a [feature flag](gl:feature-flag) that works as a kill switch, and the Notify function [rate-limits](gl:rate-limiting) marketing sends.

**What breaks.** A sandbox token sent to the production endpoint. A sign-out that leaves a shared iPad tagged `user:8812`; sign-out must remove the tag. A `.p8` key left in a repo: keep it in [Key Vault](gl:key-vault), with the hub's Full-access connection string beside it.

## Notification Hubs, Expo or FCM direct

- **Notification Hubs:** priced by tier (above). Targets with tags, expressions and templates; limited by devices per tier. Best for an Azure backend that needs segments and an SLA.
- **Expo push service:** free. You send to your own list of tokens, at up to 600 notifications a second per project. Best for Expo apps and small teams.
- **FCM direct:** free. Targets with topics and conditions, within Google's quotas. Best for apps already on Firebase.

Expo's service is simple, though it still needs your APNs key and FCM v1 credentials. At 600 a second a 400,000-install broadcast takes about eleven minutes, and every segment, opt-in and quiet-hour rule lives in your own database. FCM direct can reach iPhones too, through your APNs key. Northline picks Notification Hubs Standard: the backend is on Azure, targeting is server-side, and it carries an SLA.

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | With Notification Hubs |
|---|---|
| FCM registration token, Expo push token | Device token |
| A `push_tokens` table in your own database | Device installations |
| FCM topics, OneSignal segments, Pusher Beams interests | Tags and tag expressions |
| i18n string bundles in the app | Notification templates |
| A content-available or data-only message | Silent notification |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> In a push review, ask:
>
> 1. **Who sets the tags: the app or the API?** Tags decide who reads what, so only the backend may set them.
> 2. **What stops a send with an empty audience?** One missing filter is a broadcast to every phone.
> 3. **Is this transactional or marketing, and where is the opt-in recorded?** Apple and customers treat them very differently.
> 4. **What time is it for the person receiving it?** The hub sends now unless you decide otherwise.
> 5. **When do the APNs and FCM credentials change, and who owns them?** A broken credential silences a whole platform.

## Key takeaways

- Only APNs and FCM can reach phones. Your backend collects device tokens and hands payloads to them, through Notification Hubs at scale.
- Use APNs token auth with a `.p8` key and FCM v1 with a service account. FCM legacy is gone.
- An installation holds a device's token, tags and templates. The backend, not the app, sets tags.
- Tag expressions pick the audience; templates let one send fit every platform and language.
- Guard against broadcasts, record marketing opt-in, and respect local time.
- Standard tier for more than 200,000 devices; Expo or FCM direct when the app is small or already on Firebase.
