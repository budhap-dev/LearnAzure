## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] Twenty thousand clicks a second
> Marketing wants a live dashboard of what shoppers are doing during a sale: searches, product views, add-to-basket, abandoned checkouts. The mobile team wants app performance telemetry. The data team wants all of it in the data lake every day.
>
> Last year's attempt wrote every click into a table in the orders database. On the first sale morning it reached 20,000 inserts a second, the table's locks slowed checkout, and the DBA switched it off at 09:40. A second attempt sent clicks to a Service Bus queue, which throttled, cost more than expected, and could only be read by one consumer.
>
> Clicks are not orders. Priya needs a home for a firehose that several teams can read at once, without touching the transaction path.

## A stream is a log, not a queue

**[Azure Event Hubs](gl:azure-event-hubs)** ingests millions of events a second and keeps them for a set time. It works differently from the queues of [lesson 5.2](lesson:5.2):

| | Queue (Service Bus) | Stream (Event Hubs) |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Removes the message when it is completed | Leaves the event where it is; each reader tracks its own position |
| Readers | One receiver per message | Many independent readers of the same events |
| Replay | No - once completed, it is gone | Yes, anything still inside the retention period |
| Per-message features | Locks, dead-lettering, sessions | None: events are a sequence, not a to-do list |
| Built for | Business messages, each of which matters | Telemetry, clickstreams, logs, where volume matters |

==Use Event Hubs when the traffic is a stream of facts to be analysed, and Service Bus when each message is work that must be done.== Event Hubs also speaks the **[Apache Kafka](gl:apache-kafka)** protocol, so existing Kafka producers and consumers connect by changing their configuration, not their code.

## Partitions and keys

An event hub is split into **[partitions](gl:event-hubs-partition)**: ordered, append-only sequences that producers write to and consumers read from in parallel. More partitions means more parallel consumers, and on the Standard tier the count is **fixed when the hub is created**, so choose generously - 16 or 32 for anything that may grow.

A producer can send with a **partition key**. Every event with the same key lands in the same partition, so events for one shopper session stay in order. Choose the key the way you chose a Cosmos DB partition key in [lesson 3.3](lesson:3.3): many distinct values and an even spread. A constant key sends everything to one partition and throttles it, while the others sit idle.

## Consumer groups and checkpoints

Each application that reads the stream gets its own **[consumer group](gl:consumer-group)**: an independent view with its own position in every partition. The dashboard, the fraud check and the archive each read everything, at their own pace, without affecting each other.

Consumers record their position with a **[checkpoint](gl:checkpoint)**, usually an offset per partition saved to a Blob storage container by the SDK's event processor. After a crash or a redeploy, a consumer resumes from its last checkpoint. Everything since then is read again, so consumers are [idempotent](gl:idempotent) here too, and checkpoint every few seconds or every batch, not every event.

## Capacity and tiers

On Standard, capacity is bought in **[throughput units](gl:throughput-unit)**. One unit allows about 1 MB or 1,000 events a second in, and 2 MB a second out, for roughly $22 a month. Go over and producers get `ServerBusy` errors. **Auto-inflate** raises the unit count automatically up to a ceiling you set; it does not lower it again.

| Tier | For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | Small, single-reader streams | One consumer group, 1-day retention, no Kafka or Capture |
| **Standard** | Most workloads | Throughput units, up to 7 days' retention, Kafka, Capture as an add-on |
| **Premium** | Predictable latency, larger scale | Dedicated processing units, up to 90 days' retention, private networking |
| **Dedicated** | Very large, single-tenant estates | Whole clusters, priced by the cluster |

## Getting value out of the stream

A stream is only useful once something reads it. Four common readers:

- **[Event Hubs Capture](gl:event-hubs-capture)** writes the stream to Blob storage or the data lake as Avro or Parquet files every few minutes, with no code. That is the data team's daily feed, and a long-term archive beyond the retention period.
- **Azure Stream Analytics** runs SQL-like queries over time windows - *views per product per minute*, *baskets abandoned in the last five minutes* - and sends results to Power BI, a database or another hub.
- **Azure Functions** with an Event Hubs trigger receive events in batches, for per-event reactions such as fraud signals.
- **Microsoft Fabric Real-Time Intelligence** and Azure Data Explorer store and query streams at scale, for teams that live in analytics tools.

## In practice

The shop's design: browsers and apps send events to a small telemetry API, which batches them - browsers never hold Event Hubs credentials. The `clickstream` hub in `evh-shop-telemetry` is on Standard, with 16 partitions, 3 days' retention, partition key `sessionId`, and two throughput units auto-inflating to ten on sale days. There are three consumer groups: `dashboard` for Stream Analytics feeding Power BI, `fraud` for a function, and Capture writing Parquet to the data lake. The orders database never sees a click again.

**What it costs.** Two to ten throughput units cost about $45 to $220 a month plus a small per-million-events charge. Capture and a Stream Analytics job add perhaps $100-200 more. That is well below what last year's locked checkout cost in lost sales.

**What breaks.**

- **Too few partitions.** On Standard the count is fixed; a hub created with two partitions cannot use more than two parallel readers per consumer group.
- **A bad partition key.** A constant or low-cardinality key concentrates traffic on one partition and throttles it.
- **Two apps in one consumer group.** They fight over partitions and each sees part of the stream. One consumer group per application.
- **Retention shorter than the outage.** A consumer down for four days on three days' retention loses a day. Capture is the safety net.
- **Using a stream for commands.** No locks, no dead-lettering, no per-message acknowledgement. Orders go to Service Bus.

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> Before anyone streams data, ask:
>
> 1. **Is this a stream of facts or a list of work?** Facts to Event Hubs; work to Service Bus.
> 2. **What is the partition key, and how many partitions will we need at peak?** On Standard, decide once.
> 3. **Which consumer group does each application use, and where are its checkpoints?**
> 4. **How long could a consumer be down, and is retention longer than that?** If not, turn on Capture.
> 5. **Who sends the events, and whose credentials do they use?** Never a browser with a key.

## Key takeaways

- Event Hubs is a partitioned, retained log for high-volume streams; readers keep their own position and can replay.
- Partitions set the parallelism, and on Standard the count is fixed at creation. A good partition key keeps related events in order without hot spots.
- Each consuming application gets its own consumer group and checkpoints its progress; resuming means re-reading since the last checkpoint.
- Standard sells throughput units with auto-inflate; Premium and Dedicated sell reserved capacity and longer retention.
- Capture archives to the data lake, Stream Analytics queries windows, Functions react per batch, and the Kafka protocol lets existing clients connect.
- Streams are for facts to analyse; business work that must be acknowledged belongs on Service Bus.
