## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] "We have geo-redundant storage, so we're fine"
> A contractor runs a clean-up script against the orders database at 16:40 on a Friday. The `WHERE` clause is wrong. Forty thousand orders are gone, and the shop keeps taking new ones on top of the hole.
>
> Priya asks Dev, the ops lead, the obvious question. Dev's answer is that the database is [zone-redundant](gl:zone-redundant) and the images are on geo-redundant storage. True, and irrelevant: every replica copied the delete within milliseconds. Priya needs the database *as it was at 16:39*. Dev has never restored one on Azure and does not know how long it takes.
>
> Rule for the whole lesson: ==nobody has a backup until they have restored from it.==

## Redundancy protects hardware; backup protects data

[High availability](gl:high-availability) and [redundancy](gl:redundancy) keep the service up when a disk, a rack or a zone fails. They copy every write immediately, including the bad ones. **Backup** is a separate copy, frozen at a point in time, that survives what redundancy cannot:

| Threat | Redundancy / HA | Backup |
|---|---|---|
| Disk, node or zone failure | Yes | Not needed |
| Accidental delete, bad migration, bad deploy | No - replicated instantly | Yes, restore to before it |
| Ransomware, malicious admin | No | Yes, if the attacker cannot delete the backup |
| Whole region lost | Only with a geo replica | Yes, via geo-restore or a vault in the pair |

Two numbers from [lesson 1.7](lesson:1.7) decide every backup design: [RPO](gl:rpo), how much data you can lose, and [RTO](gl:rto), how long you can be down. A nightly backup gives an RPO of up to 24 hours however good the storage is. A backup nobody has restored has an unknown RTO, which is the same as an infinite one. [Disaster recovery](gl:disaster-recovery) is the plan that turns both numbers into steps.

## What each store already does for you

Most Azure data services back themselves up. Your job is knowing the defaults and the edges.

| Store | Automatic protection | The knob to check |
|---|---|---|
| [Azure SQL Database](gl:azure-sql-database) | Full, differential and log backups; [point-in-time restore](gl:point-in-time-restore) to any second in the last **7 days** by default, up to **35** | [Long-term retention](gl:long-term-retention) keeps weekly, monthly or yearly fulls for up to **10 years**; [geo-restore](gl:geo-restore) needs geo-redundant backup storage (the default) |
| PostgreSQL and MySQL [Flexible Server](gl:flexible-server) | Automated backups, **7-35 days** retention, zone- or geo-redundant backup storage | A deleted server keeps its backups for only **5 days** |
| [Cosmos DB](gl:cosmos-db) | **Periodic** mode: snapshots every 1-24 hours, restored via a support ticket; **continuous** mode: self-service restore to any second in **7 or 30 days** | Choose continuous; it cannot be switched back to periodic |
| Blob storage | Nothing by default. Turn on **[soft delete](gl:soft-delete)** (1-365 days), **versioning**, snapshots, and point-in-time restore for block blobs | Both cost storage; a lifecycle policy prunes old versions |
| Blob storage, legal hold | **[Immutable storage](gl:immutable-storage)** (WORM): nobody, not even an owner, can delete or overwrite until the clock runs out | A time-based policy is a commitment; test it on a throw-away container first |

The full-differential-log chain is exactly what Azure SQL runs; you just never see the `.bak` files. Backups live in separate storage replicated to the [region pair](gl:region-pair), so a geo-restore into UK West needs nothing set up in advance. It costs you the most recent log, typically an RPO of up to an hour.

## Vaults, Backup center and Site Recovery

For things that do not back themselves up you use ![Azure Backup](az:recovery-services-vault) **[Azure Backup](gl:azure-backup)**, which stores copies in one of two containers:

- A **[Recovery Services vault](gl:recovery-services-vault)** protects [virtual machines](gl:virtual-machine), SQL Server and SAP HANA inside VMs, and Azure Files.
- A **[Backup vault](gl:backup-vault)** protects blobs, [managed disks](gl:managed-disk), PostgreSQL Flexible Servers and AKS.

A **[backup policy](gl:backup-policy)** sets the schedule and the **[retention period](gl:retention-period)**: daily for 30 days, weekly for 12 weeks, monthly for 12 months is a common shape. **[Backup center](gl:backup-center)** is the single pane over every vault in every subscription: what is protected, what failed last night, what has no policy.

Two vault features target a compromised admin. **Soft delete** keeps deleted backup data for 14 days, free, so one `delete` cannot destroy your last copy. **Multi-user authorisation** needs a second person, holding a role on a separate Resource Guard, to approve turning soft delete off or shortening retention. Ransomware crews go for the backups first; make them need two stolen identities.

**[Azure Site Recovery](gl:azure-site-recovery)** is the other half of the vault: it replicates VMs continuously to a second region and fails them over with a scripted recovery plan. It is DR for IaaS, with an RPO in minutes rather than a nightly copy, at roughly $25 per protected instance per month plus replica storage.

Encryption at rest is on everywhere with Microsoft-managed keys. A **[customer-managed key](gl:customer-managed-key)** in [Key Vault](gl:key-vault) gives auditors the control they ask for, and gives you a new way to lose everything: delete or expire the key and every backup encrypted with it is unreadable. Enable purge protection on the vault before turning CMK on.

## In practice

**What it costs.** Azure Backup bills a per-instance fee plus storage: a VM under 500 GB is roughly $10 a month, then backup storage at a few cents per GB-month, more for geo-redundant. Azure SQL includes backup storage equal to the database's maximum size; beyond that, and for all LTR, you pay per GB-month at the backup storage rate. Soft delete and versioning are just more blobs on the bill. A restore test costs one engineer one afternoon; the alternative costs the company.

**What breaks.**

- **Retention shorter than time-to-notice.** A bad migration found after five weeks is unrecoverable with 35-day PITR. That is what LTR is for.
- **Backups in the blast radius.** Locally redundant backup storage in the region that just went dark is decoration.
- **Deleting the parent deletes the backups.** Delete an Azure SQL *database* and its PITR backups survive for the retention window; delete the logical server or the [resource group](gl:resource-group) and only LTR survives. A deleted Flexible Server keeps backups for 5 days. A [resource lock](gl:resource-lock) on `rg-shop-prod` is cheaper than learning this.
- **The key is gone.** TDE with a customer-managed key that someone tidied out of Key Vault.
- **Nobody has ever restored.** The RTO is a guess, the runbook has a step that no longer works, and the person who knew left in March.

**The [restore test](gl:restore-test) playbook.** Run it against production backups, into a throw-away resource group, every quarter:

| Step | Do | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pick a scenario | "Contractor deleted orders at 16:40 Friday" | The scenario and the target point in time |
| 2. Restore to a new name | PITR `sqldb-orders-prod` to `sqldb-orders-drill` at 16:39 | Start and finish timestamps |
| 3. Validate | Row counts, latest order id, app smoke test against the copy | Query output, screenshot |
| 4. Time it | Include finding the button and the approvals | Measured RTO versus the promised one |
| 5. Repeat for blobs and the VM | Undelete a blob, restore a VM to a test network | Same as above |
| 6. Fix and record | Update the runbook, delete the drill resources | A dated entry and the next drill booked |

## Map it to what you know

| You already know | On Azure |
|---|---|
| Nightly full plus 15-minute log backups in a maintenance plan | Azure SQL automated backups; the chain is the same, you never see the files |
| `RESTORE DATABASE ... STOPAT` | Point-in-time restore, always to a new database name |
| Copying `.bak` files to tape and off-site | Long-term retention; geo-redundant backup storage is the off-site |
| The Recycle Bin | Soft delete, for blobs, for databases, and for the vault itself |
| Git history for a file | Blob versioning; immutable storage is a branch nobody can force-push |
| A warm standby server in the second data centre | Site Recovery for VMs; failover groups for Azure SQL ([lesson 3.2](lesson:3.2)) |

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM] The quarterly restore drill
> Ask these out loud, once a quarter, and write the answers down:
>
> 1. **When did we last restore each store from a real backup, and how long did it take?** "Never" is an answer, and it goes in the risk register.
> 2. **Is retention longer than our time-to-notice?** If a bug can hide for two months, 35 days is not enough.
> 3. **Where do the backups live?** "The same region" or "the same resource group, no lock" gets fixed this sprint.
> 4. **Who can delete a backup, and does it take two people?** Soft delete on; multi-user authorisation on for anything customer-facing.
> 5. **If we lose the encryption key, can we still read the backup?** Purge protection on Key Vault and a documented key-recovery path.
> 6. **Does the DR region have somewhere for restored data to run?** A database in UK West with no API pointed at it is not recovery.

## Key takeaways

- Redundancy replicates every write, including the bad ones; backup is a separate copy at a point in time. You need both.
- Azure SQL: 7 days of PITR by default, up to 35, LTR up to 10 years. Flexible Server: 7-35 days. Cosmos DB continuous: any second in 7 or 30 days. Blobs: nothing until you turn on soft delete and versioning.
- Recovery Services vaults hold VM, SQL-in-VM and Files backups; Backup vaults hold blobs, disks and PostgreSQL; Backup center is the single view; Site Recovery is region-level DR for VMs.
- Vault soft delete and multi-user authorisation exist because attackers delete backups before they encrypt data.
- Retention must outlast time-to-notice, backups must sit outside the blast radius, and the key that encrypts them must be unloseable.
- Your RTO is what the last drill measured. Nobody has a backup until they have restored from it.
