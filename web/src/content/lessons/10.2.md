## Why this matters

> [!SCENARIO] One notebook, everyone throttled
> Northline's first Fabric capacity is an F8 in UK South, bought pay-as-you-go so nobody had to commit. For three weeks it is quiet. Then Mei, a new marketing analyst, starts a Spark notebook at 22:00 on Monday: eighteen months of clickstream joined to every order, re-read from scratch in each cell. It finishes overnight.
>
> At 09:15 on Tuesday finance's trading pack will not open. Visuals spin, then fail with "Your organization's Fabric compute capacity has exceeded its limits". The finance director asks how one analyst's homework can switch off the board's reports. Priya asks a sharper question: what should this capacity cost, and who is allowed to spend it?

## What Fabric is

**[Microsoft Fabric](gl:microsoft-fabric)** is Microsoft's analytics platform delivered as software as a service. Pipelines, lakehouses, warehouses, notebooks, real-time analytics and Power BI share one storage layer and one bill. It replaces kit you used to assemble yourself: a storage account, Data Factory, Synapse and Power BI Premium, each with its own invoice. Power BI Premium's P SKUs are being retired in favour of Fabric's F SKUs.

Four nouns carry the model:

- **The [tenant](gl:tenant)**: your [Microsoft Entra ID](gl:microsoft-entra-id) organisation. It has exactly one OneLake.
- **A [Fabric capacity](gl:fabric-capacity)**: compute you buy in Azure, sized in **[capacity units](gl:capacity-unit)** (CUs). An F8 has 8 CUs; sizes run from F2 to F2048 and beyond, each double the last.
- **A [Fabric workspace](gl:fabric-workspace)**: a container for items, with its own roles (Admin, Member, Contributor, Viewer), assigned to one capacity.
- **Items**: lakehouses, warehouses, notebooks, pipelines and reports.

## Capacity: smoothing and throttling

Every report click and Spark job burns CU-seconds. A capacity **bursts**, running work faster than its size allows, then pays the cost back over time. That is **[smoothing and throttling](gl:smoothing-and-throttling)**. Interactive work, such as report queries, is smoothed over 5 to 64 minutes. Background work, such as refreshes, pipelines and Spark, is smoothed over 24 hours. Overspend, and the capacity borrows from its own future:

| Future capacity already used | What happens |
|---|---|
| Up to 10 minutes | Nothing: overage protection |
| 10 to 60 minutes | New interactive requests wait 20 seconds |
| 60 minutes to 24 hours | New interactive requests are rejected; background jobs still run |
| More than 24 hours | Everything new is rejected until the debt is paid |

That was Northline's Tuesday. Mei's notebook was background work, so its cost was spread over the next 24 hours on top of the normal load. By morning the capacity was in debt, and finance's reports were rejected. Nothing was broken.

==Throttling is per capacity, so the reliable way to protect a critical workload is to give it its own capacity.==

To recover, wait for the debt to burn down, scale up for a few hours, or pause and resume. Pausing clears the debt at once, but bills the outstanding usage and takes everything on that capacity offline. New capacities also have **capacity overage** on by default. It pays for usage above the SKU instead of throttling, up to a daily limit you set, at three times the pay-as-you-go rate. **Surge protection** rejects new background jobs before the capacity reaches deep throttling. A version that caps a single workspace is in preview.

## OneLake and shortcuts

**[OneLake](gl:onelake)** is the one [data lake](gl:data-lake) every Fabric tenant gets automatically: OneDrive for analytics data, organised by workspace. It is built on Azure Data Lake Storage, so tools that use the ADLS Gen2 API can read it. Data sits in the region of the workspace's capacity, which matters for [data residency](gl:data-residency).

Every engine stores tables in one open format. **[Parquet](gl:parquet)** files hold the data column by column, compressed. **[Delta Lake](gl:delta-lake)** adds a transaction log beside them, which turns a folder of Parquet into a [lakehouse](gl:lakehouse) table with transactions and updates. T-SQL, Spark and Power BI all read the same Delta tables.

A **[OneLake shortcut](gl:onelake-shortcut)** makes data stored elsewhere appear as a folder or table, with no copy. Targets include other workspaces, ADLS Gen2, Blob Storage, Amazon S3, Google Cloud Storage, Dataverse, SharePoint, and on-premises stores through a gateway. Northline's clickstream Parquet, written by [Event Hubs Capture](gl:event-hubs-capture) into `stshopanalytics`, appears in bronze through a shortcut instead of a nightly copy.

==A shortcut is a pointer, not a copy: one set of files, still controlled by its owner.==

Know its edges. Move or rename the target and the shortcut breaks. Deleting a file through a shortcut deletes it at the source, if you have write access. Reading from another cloud incurs that cloud's [egress](gl:egress) charges; optional shortcut caching reduces them.

## In practice

**What it costs.** Approximate pay-as-you-go list prices in UK South are about $0.21 per CU per hour, and they vary by region. Running all month, that is roughly $310 for an F2, $1,230 for an F8 and $9,800 for an F64. A one-year [reservation](gl:reservation) cuts about 40%, but unused reserved hours are lost, so pausing a reserved capacity saves nothing. OneLake storage is billed separately, at about the price of hot blob storage, even while paused.

**The F64 line.** Below F64, everyone who views Power BI content needs a paid licence; Power BI Pro lists at about $14 per user per month. From F64, viewers with the free Fabric licence and a Viewer role can read reports. Authors need Pro at any size. Northline's 41 Pro licences cost about $575 a month; a reserved F64 costs about $5,800. It pays for itself on licences only at roughly 400 to 700 viewers.

Northline's setup after Tuesday:

- **Production: F8, reserved,** for the Sales, Finance and Marketing workspaces. Overage on, with a small daily limit and an alert to Dev.
- **Sandbox: F2, pay-as-you-go,** for notebooks, paused at night and weekends by a runbook. If it overspends, only the sandbox throttles.
- **Workspaces per domain and environment**, such as `Sales [Dev]` and `Sales [Prod]`, each with a named owner. Changes move through deployment pipelines or Git, never by hand.

**Trial and free options.** The Fabric trial is free for 60 days, as an F4 or F64-sized capacity with 1 TB of OneLake storage, without Copilot or Private Link. It is for evaluation only: afterwards, Fabric items become unusable and can be deleted after seven days unless moved to a paid capacity. A second trial is not guaranteed. The free Fabric licence is per user, not a capacity. The cheapest paid route is an F2, paused when idle.

## Guiding your team

> [!TEAM]
> Before anyone buys or assigns a capacity:
>
> 1. **Which workloads share this capacity?** Throttling is per capacity, so experiments must not sit beside the board's reports.
> 2. **What is the base load, and what is the peak?** Reserve the base and pay as you go above it, because unused reserved hours are lost.
> 3. **Is capacity overage on, and with what daily limit?** It bills at three times the normal rate.
> 4. **How many people view reports?** Below F64 each needs Pro, but F64 is a lot to spend on licences alone.
> 5. **Could this be a shortcut instead of a copy?** Every copy is another pipeline, another bill and another hiding place for personal data.

## Key takeaways

- Fabric is SaaS analytics: one OneLake per tenant, compute bought as capacities in CUs, and workspaces that each run on one capacity.
- Capacities burst and smooth. Overspend, and interactive requests are delayed, then rejected, then everything is rejected.
- Give critical reports their own capacity, reserve the steady base, pause pay-as-you-go capacities when idle, and set overage on purpose.
- Below F64 every Power BI viewer needs a paid licence; from F64, free-licence viewers can read. Do the arithmetic first.
- Delta tables over Parquet are the shared format, and shortcuts reference data elsewhere instead of copying it. The 60-day trial is for evaluation, not production.
